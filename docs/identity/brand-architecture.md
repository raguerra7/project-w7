# Purpose

This document defines how W7, as the master brand, relates to the initiatives created under it. It intentionally does not define visual identity (typography, color, voice and tone) — those are deferred until a specific initiative has an actual visual asset to design around. See [Related Documents](#related-documents) for why.

---

# Context

A brand architecture answers a narrower question than "what does this look like": it answers "when someone encounters an initiative, how do they understand its relationship to W7?" That question needs an answer before any initiative gets far enough to need a logo, because retrofitting a naming and ownership model onto an initiative that has already grown its own identity is far more disruptive than defining it first.

---

# The Model: Endorsed Sub-Brands

W7 uses an **endorsed brand architecture**: each initiative has its own name and its own freedom to develop a distinct voice and visual expression, while carrying a visible, consistent connection back to W7 as the ecosystem it belongs to.

This is a deliberate middle point between two extremes:

- **A single monolithic brand** (everything is simply "W7 Something"), which would force every initiative — a technical tips blog, a future consulting practice, a future academy — into one voice and one audience, when they may not share either.
- **Fully independent brands** with no visible connection to W7, which would prevent the ecosystem from compounding: someone who trusts WarTips would have no way to discover, or reason to trust, whatever comes next.

WarTips is the reference example: it is its own name, with its own voice (see its [initiative registration](../initiatives/wartips/README.md)), while visibly operating as a W7 initiative.

---

# What Is Shared Across Every Initiative

- The name "W7" as the visible parent, referenced from every initiative's own documentation or public presence.
- The philosophy in the [Manifesto](../foundations/manifesto.md) and [Constitution](../foundations/constitution.md) — initiatives do not restate it differently or contradict it.
- The closing philosophy, "Enjoy the Journey," used consistently as W7's own signature, distinct from any initiative's own voice.

# What Each Initiative Defines for Itself

- Its own name and how prominently it stands relative to the W7 parent reference.
- Its own tone, appropriate to its own audience — WarTips speaks differently than a future Academy initiative would, and that is by design, not an inconsistency to correct.
- Its own visual expression, once one is warranted — see below.

---

# Why Visual Identity Is Deferred

Typography, color systems and detailed voice-and-tone guidance are normally part of a brand architecture document. They are deliberately not included here: W7 currently has no public-facing visual asset for any of these to apply to, and writing detailed visual specifications before there is a real design to encode would mean guessing, then very likely rewriting once an actual initiative needs one. When an initiative reaches the point of needing a real visual identity, that need — and the resulting decision — is expected to be recorded as an ADR and to produce the concrete `docs/identity/` documents this one currently leaves out.
